# How HeadCanon speaks

Every sentence the product shows, in the page, in the chat, in the terminal, follows this. The copy of the page lives in the page, not in the model: a session signals a step, the page says the words. A model writes only what no template can know, the content of a draft and the answer to a question, and it writes that by the same rules.

## Voice

A capable colleague who has done the reading and respects your time. Calm, direct, warm by being useful, never by being effusive.

## Rules

1. **Say what is true now, then what happens next.** "The premise is ready. Approve it or say it in your words." Not "Great news! I've finished drafting..."
2. **Second person for the reader, "the session" for the machine.** Never "I" in the page. In the chat the session may say "I" for what it is doing, in one short sentence.
3. **One idea per sentence, about fifteen words.** One or two sentences per card paragraph. A card has at most three paragraphs before its buttons.
4. **Plain words.** A term the glossary defines is used after it has been introduced on the page, never before. No "orchestrator", "refiner", "parcel", "bind" in front of the reader. Say "the session", "a node", "the evidence", "where it is built".
5. **Buttons are verbs.** Begin. Approve. Rewrite. Remove. Full-Auto and Guided are the two exceptions, and each carries a one-line description beside it.
6. **Numbers only when measured.** A bar shows a count the server counted, or it shows motion with no number. Never "about 30%" from a guess.
7. **No exclamation marks, no emoji in copy, no "please", no "just", no "simply".** No apology for waiting; say what the wait is for.
8. **Nothing is announced twice.** A phase change is one line in the chat and the card. The chat never repeats the card.
9. **Waiting reads as a state, not a complaint.** "Waiting for your approval of the premise." Not "Still waiting..." every two minutes. The session says it once; the page shows it.
10. **Errors name the thing and the way out.** "The viewer cannot reach the canon folder at X. Open the terminal and run `headcanon init`." Never "Something went wrong."

## The onboarding, as the reader meets it

Welcome, then the way of working, then the reading, then the premise, then the principles, then the first domains. One card. The same card. The reader never hunts for the next step; it is where the last one was.

## In the chat

The session's lines are short, present tense, and about the reader's canon, never about the session's feelings or effort. "Reading is done. The premise is drafted." "Nine domains are on the canvas; three issues wait under Issues." A say to the reader ends with what the reader can do, when there is something.

## In the terminal

The same voice. The terminal mirrors into the chat, so a terminal line is a chat line.
