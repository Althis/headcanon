# Do requirement hierarchies keep siblings mutually exclusive?

Research note, 2026-10-11. Question: do established frameworks that decompose requirements, goals or capabilities hierarchically require sibling nodes to be mutually exclusive, and how do they handle overlap and cross-cutting concerns? Gathered by a research subagent from primary sources; claims carry their URL. Items marked "not confirmed" could not be read at the source.

## Findings by framework

**KAOS (van Lamsweerde).** A goal model is "a directed graph (which is more general than a simple tree)", so "a given goal can appear on different diagrams to refine different higher-level goals" (Respect-IT KAOS Tutorial 2007, https://www.cse.msu.edu/~cse870/Materials/GoalModeling/KaosTutorial-2007.pdf). AND-refinement means "satisfying all subgoals in the refinement is sufficient for satisfying the parent goal"; the semantics is sufficiency, never disjointness (van Lamsweerde, RE'01, https://webperso.info.ucl.ac.be/~avl/files/RE01.pdf). Overlap and tension are first-class: conflict links, obstacles ("an obstacle O obstructs a goal G") with resolution tactics. No exclusivity rule exists.

**i\* / Tropos.** Goal graphs with means-ends and decomposition links; iStar 2.0 constrains only that an element is the parent of at most one refinement and that refinement has no cycles (https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07767). Cross-cutting qualities are a separate element type reached by contribution links (make, help, hurt, break).

**Feature models (FODA and successors).** FODA's diagram "is an and/or tree" with cross-tree constraints "requires" and "mutually exclusive with", where mutual exclusion is a configuration constraint, not non-overlap of scope (SEI CMU/SEI-90-TR-21, https://www.sei.cmu.edu/documents/1011/1990_005_001_15872.pdf). Later notations are directed acyclic graphs, and the formal comparison finds that "tree languages are not fully expressive" while "DAG languages are fully expressive" (Trigaux et al., https://pure.unamur.be/ws/files/1016783/58154.pdf).

**Work breakdown structures.** A strict tree with the 100% rule: every level "must contain all of the deliverable elements, which represent 100% of its parent" (PMI, https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/work-breakdown-structure-basics-5919). NASA's handbook puts the no-overlap rule in the WBS dictionary, prepared "to eliminate duplication and overlap of work assignments" (https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200000300/downloads/20200000300.pdf). Shared work is handled by mandated "common elements" replicated under each element they support (MIL-STD-881D §3.2.6, https://www.humphreys-assoc.com/evms/evms-documents/dod/DOD_MIL-STD-881D_9_April_2018.pdf). The PMI Practice Standard's own exclusivity wording: not confirmed.

**Business capability maps.** The Open Group guide requires a "complete, stable set of business capabilities" and never uses the words mutually exclusive (G189, https://governance.foundation/assets/frameworks/togaf/g189%20-%20Business%20Capbility.pdf); overlap with value streams is handled by cross-mapping matrices and a Supporting tier. Vendor guidance does demand exclusivity and reports it as "one of the most common issues in both first-time mapping efforts and remapping projects" (LeanIX, https://www.leanix.net/en/wiki/ea/best-practices-to-define-business-capabilities). A practitioner lists the three options when one ability is needed in two legs: duplicate it, assign it arbitrarily, or move it to a generic leg (Berrisford, http://grahamberrisford.com/00EAframeworks/03TOGAF/AMTOGpapers/Business%20architecture%20(inc%20capabilities%20and%20value%20streams)%20in%20TOGAF.htm).

**SysML requirements.** Containment is a strict tree, and the specification says why that is a problem: "the use of namespace containment to specify requirements hierarchies precludes reusing requirements in different contexts since a given model element can only exist in one namespace". It adds a Copy relationship for reuse and the many-to-many derive, satisfy, verify, refine and trace dependencies (OMG SysML 1.6 §16, https://www.omg.org/spec/SysML/1.6/PDF).

**User story mapping.** A narrative grid ordered by time: "the order you'd explain the behavior of the system in is the correct order" (Patton, https://www.jpattonassociates.com/the-new-backlog/). No exclusivity rule found; handling of a task serving two activities: not confirmed.

**DOORS and Jama.** A single-parent outline plus typed links whose cardinality is chosen per link module, including many-to-many (https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/engineering-lifecycle-management-suite/doors/9.7.2?topic=pairings-creating-link-modules). DOORS Next lets an artifact live in several modules with edits propagating (https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SSYMRC_7.0.2/com.ibm.rational.rrm.help.doc/topics/t_reuse_artifacts_modules.html); Jama synchronizes copies under a shared global ID (https://help.jamasoftware.com/en/manage-content/reuse-and-synchronization/reuse.html).

**MECE (Minto).** A grouping rule for pyramids: "you can't derive an idea from a grouping unless the ideas in the grouping are logically the same" (https://www.mckinsey.com/alumni/news-and-insights/global-news/alumni-news/barbara-minto-mece-i-invented-it-so-i-get-to-say-how-to-pronounce-it). Critics replace exclusive with independent and allow intentional redundancy (https://powerful-problem-solving.com/dont-overdo-mece). The software-specific critique is the "tyranny of the dominant decomposition": any single axis leaves concerns that "overlap, and can affect one another" (Tarr et al., ICSE 1999, https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~gregor/teaching/papers/p107-tarr.pdf), and functional requirements that cut across the base decomposition "are not effectively handled" (Moreira et al., RE'05).

**Volere and ISO/IEC 25010.** Volere's requirement shell links one requirement to several use cases ("Event/BUC/PUC #: 7, 9") and carries Dependencies and Conflicts fields; non-functional requirements are separate sections (https://www.cs.uic.edu/~i442/VolereMaterials/templateArchive16/c%20Volere%20template16.pdf). ISO/IEC 25010 is a two-level quality taxonomy, not a decomposition method.

## Synthesis

Strict sibling exclusivity is a hard rule only where the tree is an accounting device, work breakdown structures and presentation pyramids, and even there the standards legislate escape hatches. Every requirements or goal framework proper is either a directed acyclic graph or a tree with sanctioned leaks. The common escape hatches, in order of frequency: typed cross-links; a dedicated shared or supporting branch, lifted or replicated; controlled duplication with synchronization; a second taxonomy for quality concerns, whose known cost is that cross-cutting functional requirements go unhandled.

What the research did not settle: how to choose where a branch is cut, the dimension of decomposition. No framework above prescribes it; they prescribe completeness and the handling of overlap once a cut exists.
